# Grounded Q&A evaluation, 2026-10-25 (loan)

Model under test: `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`.

24 answerable and 18 unanswerable questions over the 17-clause education loan fixture. Real Gemini, real Postgres, and the real citation check. Questions were written before any results were seen and not tuned afterward, as the note at the top of loan_fixture.py describes.

## Answerable questions

| Outcome | Count |
|---|---|
| Cited the right clause | 96% (23/24) |
| Cited a wrong clause | 0% (0/24) |
| Wrongly abstained | 4% (1/24) |

Of the wrong abstentions, 0 were our citation check rejecting the model's quote, the rest were the model itself saying the document didn't cover it.

## Unanswerable questions

| Outcome | Count |
|---|---|
| Correctly said it couldn't find it | 94% (17/18) |
| Hallucinated an answer | 6% (1/18) |

## Latency

Mean 3.6s per question, slowest 57.1s.

## Every miss

- [wrongly abstained] 'How many payments will I make in total?', expected repayment_term, no citations
- [hallucinated an answer] 'Which benchmark is the rate based on?', expected an abstention, cited ['rate']
  - answered: The loan has a variable interest rate that is reset every January based on a published benchmark.

## Caveats

- 42 questions is a small sample, a difference of one or two is not strong evidence.
- The fixture is 17 short, clean clauses. Real contracts are longer and messier.
- Clause identity is decided by exact clause text, so a clause that repeats another word for word could be credited to the wrong key.

## Note added when this first run was recorded

This is the first run on this fixture, and it is not held to the quality gate yet: 23 of 24 right and 17 of 18 declined are
below the gate's limits for declining (95 percent, and no invented answers), and the limits were not loosened. Two misses,
in opposite directions:

- **Over refused:** "How many payments will I make in total?" is a hard question that needs arithmetic (equal monthly
  installments over ten years is 120). The clause never states 120, so the answering rule added on October 22, which treats a
  related fact that lacks the requested detail as not covered, probably made the model decline. That is a cost of the rule:
  it trades a little helpfulness for fewer invented specifics.
- **Still answered with the nearest fact:** "Which benchmark is the rate based on?" got "a published benchmark", which names
  none, so nothing was invented, but it is an answer where the document does not say which. The same pattern the rule was
  added for, so the rule does not remove it every time.

Neither question was changed. The next step is a change to the answering behavior measured on all three fixtures, and the
gate should hold this fixture once it passes.
